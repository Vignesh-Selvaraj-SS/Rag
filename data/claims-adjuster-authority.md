# Claims Procedure CP-09 — Adjuster Authority, Reserving and Denials

**Document:** CP-09, revision 4
**Effective:** 15 February 2026
**Owner:** Claims Operations, Meridian Mutual

---

## 1. Settlement authority limits

No adjuster may settle above their authority. Authority applies to the **total incurred**
on the claim, not to a single payment.

| Role | Settlement authority | Reserve authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Desk adjuster, grade 1 | $10,000 | $15,000 |
| Desk adjuster, grade 2 | $25,000 | $40,000 |
| Field adjuster | $75,000 | $100,000 |
| Senior field adjuster | $150,000 | $200,000 |
| Claims team manager | $350,000 | $500,000 |
| Large Loss unit manager | $1,000,000 | $1,500,000 |
| Head of Claims | Policy limits | Policy limits |

Splitting a loss into multiple claims or multiple payments to stay within authority is a
**disciplinary matter** and is detected by the weekly authority-breach report.

## 2. Reserving

An initial reserve must be set **within two business days** of the adjuster's first
contact. Reserves are set at the **most likely outcome**, not the worst case and not the
amount already estimated.

Reserves must be reviewed and updated:

- within **5 business days** of any inspection or new estimate
- within **2 business days** of receiving a contractor supplement
- at minimum **every 30 days** while the claim is open
- immediately where new information changes the expected outcome by more than **25%**

Separate reserve lines are required for: dwelling, other structures, contents, loss of use,
and each attached endorsement responding to the loss. Expense reserves for independent
adjusters, engineers and legal fees are recorded separately from indemnity.

## 3. Deductible application

The deductible is applied **once per occurrence**, not once per coverage. Where an
endorsement carries its own deductible, that deductible replaces the base deductible for
the portion of the loss falling under the endorsement:

- Water backup (HO-2026-01): **$500**, replacing the base $1,000
- Service line (HO-2026-02): **$500**, replacing the base $1,000
- Equipment breakdown (HO-2026-03): **$500**, replacing the base $1,000
- Scheduled property (HO-2026-04): **no deductible**
- Identity fraud (HO-2026-07): **$250**

Where a single occurrence triggers both a base-form loss and an endorsement loss, apply the
**base deductible once to the base-form portion** and the endorsement deductible once to
the endorsement portion. Do not apply the base deductible to the endorsement portion as
well.

## 4. Denials

A denial requires:

1. A **coverage position reviewed by a second adjuster** at or above the deciding
   adjuster's grade, recorded in the file before the letter goes out;
2. A written denial letter quoting the **exact policy or endorsement wording** relied on,
   with the form number and the paragraph reference;
3. A plain-language explanation of why that wording applies to these facts;
4. The **appeal route and time limit**, and the regulator's contact details;
5. Retention of all evidence relied on.

Denial letters must be issued within **10 business days** of the coverage decision. A
denial may not cite a general exclusion without identifying the specific paragraph.

**Partial denials** — where part of a loss is paid and part is declined — follow the same
process. Paying the undisputed portion does not waive the right to decline the disputed
portion, provided the reservation of rights was issued first.

## 5. Reservation of rights

A reservation of rights letter is issued where coverage is in doubt but investigation
continues. It must be issued **before** any payment is made under the claim, must state the
specific provisions in question, and must be reissued if new provisions come into question.

## 6. Salvage and subrogation

Subrogation must be evaluated on every claim above **$5,000**. Referral to the Recovery
unit is mandatory where a third party may be responsible, including:

- service line failures caused by a **utility or contractor** excavation
- equipment breakdown caused by a **manufacturing defect** or an off-premises power surge
- water backup caused by a **municipal sewer main** failure
- any loss where a contractor was working on the premises at the time

Preserve the failed component and photograph it in place before removal. Failure to
preserve evidence that would have supported recovery is recorded as a **recovery leakage**
event in the file audit.

## 7. File audit standards

Every claim is subject to audit against these standards. The five most common audit
failures, in order of frequency, are:

1. Reserve not updated within 30 days
2. Denial letter without a specific paragraph citation
3. Depreciation applied before the deductible on roof claims under HO-2026-08
4. Subrogation not evaluated on a claim above $5,000
5. Reservation of rights issued after the first payment rather than before
